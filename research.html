Research

I’m a PhD student in Computer Science at Kent State University, where I work with Dr. Gokarna Sharma in the SCALE Lab. I study how distributed systems can use imperfect predictions about future requests. My MS research focused on finding shared objects in a network. I’m now also studying how to route multiple repairpersons when new requests arrive over time.

Learning-augmented distributed directories

When a node writes to a shared object, ownership moves to that node. A later reader needs to find the current owner to get a copy. My collaborators and I asked whether predicting which nodes will request the object can reduce the distance that directory messages travel, even when some predictions are wrong.

Our IEEE NCA 2025 paper studied the case where all predicted requesters are known at the outset, but their request times are unknown. In my MS thesis and our SSS 2026 paper, Online Learning-Augmented Distributed Directories, we studied the case where predictions themselves arrive over time.

I studied two protocols. The first connects the initially predicted nodes and the owner with a Steiner tree, attaches the remaining nodes by shortest paths, and keeps that tree fixed. The second organizes nodes into clusters. An initially predicted node can become a leader when a request path visits its cluster.

I evaluated both protocols on random, small-world, Internet-like, and grid networks. I measured stretch by comparing the total distance along the overlay with the total shortest-path distance in the original network for the same sequence of holders, starting with the owner. This let me examine how much extra distance the protocols incurred as prediction error varied.

Online routing with multiple repairpersons

I’m studying the online traveling repairperson problem on a line. Each request identifies a location, and the algorithm learns about it only when it is released. With several repairpersons, the algorithm must decide who serves each request and in what order. I’m analyzing how to make those decisions using only the requests known so far.

Course project in distributed subgraph matching

For my Big Data Analytics course, I built a Hadoop MapReduce pipeline to find occurrences of a query graph inside a larger graph. Before verifying a match, the pipeline compares signatures that summarize the neighborhoods around candidate vertices. This rules out structurally incompatible candidates before the more expensive verification step.

Undergraduate thesis on sign gesture recognition

At Tribhuvan University, I trained a convolutional neural network to recognize hand gestures representing the letters A through Z. I used OpenCV to process the images and TensorFlow to train the classifier. My collaborators and I published the conference paper (PDF), and I presented a poster at the 12th IOE Graduate Conference in Nepal in 2022.
